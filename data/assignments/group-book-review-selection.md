# Discussion Board: Book Selection & Group Formation

## Purpose

For this discussion, you will:

* Explore the list of suggested books
* Identify topics that interest you
* Connect with classmates who want to read the same (or similar) book
* Begin forming your project group

Your goal is to find a group of **3–4 students** who share a common interest.

## Step 1: Initial Post

Create a post that includes the following:

### 1. Book Interest

List **1–3 books** you are most interested in reading.

Example:

* *Masters of Doom* – interested in early FPS development and modding
* *Gaming Representation* – interested in identity and diversity in games

### 2. Why These Books?

In 3–5 sentences, explain:

* What draws you to these books
* What topics interest you (history, representation, design, industry, etc.)
* Any connections to your own gaming experience

### 3. Availability / Preferences (Optional but Helpful)

You may include:

* Preferred meeting style (in-person, online, flexible)
* General availability (e.g., evenings, weekends)
* Any relevant skills (editing video, writing, organizing, etc.)

## Step 2: Replies & Group Formation

Reply to classmates whose interests align with yours.

In your replies:

* Express interest in forming a group
* Identify a shared book (or narrow down options)
* Begin coordinating

Once you have **3–4 students**, reply in the thread to confirm your group.

## Step 3: Final Group Post (One Member Submits)

One person from your group should make a final reply that includes:

* Group member names
* Final book selection
* Any alternate book (optional)

## Guidelines

* You are not required to choose from the provided list, but all books must be approved
* Groups must have **3–4 members**
* Try to join a group by the deadline—don’t wait too long to respond
* Be respectful and proactive when reaching out

## Instructor Notes

I may:

* Help match students who have not yet found a group
* Adjust groups if needed
* Approve alternate book choices

## Tip

The best groups form quickly and communicate early. If you see someone interested in the same book—reach out.
