### The Birth of BASIC

**EXPLORE FURTHER**

YouTube

[https://youtu.be/WYPNjSoDrqw?si=TVzqzV7JnbDy\_VUn](<https://youtu.be/WYPNjSoDrqw?si=TVzqzV7JnbDy_VUn>)

Not directly about games, but ties into my story. This video details the creation of the BASIC programming language at Dartmouth College 50 years ago, driven by the "nutty idea" that all undergraduate students should be computer literate. Professors John Kemeny and Thomas Kurtz developed the language and the **Dartmouth Time-Sharing System (DTSS)** to provide students with immediate, simple access to computing resources. Remarkably, the complex software was primarily written by undergraduate students, proving that simple high-level languages could be compiled and run simultaneously by multiple users. A few highlights:

* Before getting their own computer, Dartmouth had to transport punch cards to MIT every two weeks via train, resulting in a data transfer rate of only **1.67 bits per second**.
* To pay for their first computer (the LGP-30) without a budget, the college classified it as "furniture" to use funds set aside for furnishings.

### 1981: How Computer Graphics will Change the World

**EXPLORE FURTHER**

YouTube

[https://youtu.be/W8-54-9J9ns?si=GtVJ2EWE-1PZGWfg](<https://youtu.be/W8-54-9J9ns?si=GtVJ2EWE-1PZGWfg>)

A fun historical look at early predictions about graphics technology, this BBC Horizon documentary from 1981 traces the origins and rapid evolution of computer graphics, beginning with early military applications like the Whirlwind computer and Ivan Sutherland's pioneering "Sketchpad". It explores the development of early video games like **Pong** and **Space War**, while also highlighting how graphics technology was being utilized in flight simulators, 3D animation, and molecular biology to model DNA. The video emphasizes that the "magic" of realistic computer imagery largely flowered at the **University of Utah**, where students developed mathematical formulas for shading, texture, and curved surfaces. A few highlights:

* **Pong** was so successful in its initial consumer test that the machine "broke" simply because its coin box was completely filled with quarters.
* Early 3D animation was so labor-intensive that a single frame could take **7 minutes** to compute, and some conventional animators initially refused to work with computers.

### The Definitive Guide to Video Game Genres and Game Types

**EXPLORE FURTHER**

Article | 60 min

[https://codakid.com/video-game-genres/](<https://codakid.com/video-game-genres/>)

Helpful for distinguishing game genres. A bit long, so consider skimming or bookmarking and using as a reference. This comprehensive encyclopedia defines and categorizes the vast array of video game genres and subgenres used in the industry today. It provides detailed descriptions of core categories like Action, RPG, and Strategy, while also exploring specialized types such as "Idle Games" and "Advergames". The guide serves as an educational tool, explaining how specific gameplay mechanics—like the "blobber" movement in first-person party RPGs—define different categories. A few highlights:

* **4X strategy games** are defined by four specific goals: **Explore, Expand, Exploit, and Exterminate**.
* **"Exergames"** (fitness games) became particularly important during the COVID-19 pandemic as a tool for rehabilitation and physical activity.
* **Advergames** are promotional tools created specifically to market a brand, with examples including games for **McDonald's and Chipotle**.

### The Game Beat: Observations and Lessons from Two Decades Writing about Games by Kyle Orland

**EXPLORE FURTHER**

Book

[https://press.etc.cmu.edu/books/game-beat](<https://press.etc.cmu.edu/books/game-beat>)

*The Game Beat* is a collection of over 80 columns by journalist Kyle Orland that examines the history and ethics of the gaming press. Published through Carnegie Mellon University's ETC Press, the book explores the evolution of video game journalism over two decades, covering the transition from print magazines to digital media.
