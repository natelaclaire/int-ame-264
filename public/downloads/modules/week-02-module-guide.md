### The Arcade Project: A Cultural History of Gaming Spaces

**EXPLORE FURTHER**

YouTube | ~70 min

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkm8\_2hb9VQ&ab\_channel=CSULBCenterfortheHistoryofVideoGamesandCriticalPlay](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=nkm8_2hb9VQ&ab_channel=CSULBCenterfortheHistoryofVideoGamesandCriticalPlay>)

The Digital Arcade Project examines how racial segregation and urban geography shaped the accessibility of gaming spaces in Los Angeles County between 1977 and 1987. By layering arcade locations over 1980 census data, the researchers reveal that high-end "primary" and "secondary" venues were intentionally situated in white suburban peripheries and along freeway corridors, effectively isolating communities of color. This physical distance was reinforced by discriminatory zoning laws and a popular media narrative that framed arcades as wholesome, middle-class environments while branding inner-city "quaternary" play spaces as illicit or dangerous. Ultimately, the study demonstrates how the distribution of video games functioned as a form of fiscal and social discrimination, reflecting broader historical efforts to regulate and restrict the leisure of non-white populations.
