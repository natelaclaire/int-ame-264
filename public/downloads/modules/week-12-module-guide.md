### The Evolution Of The LAN Party

**REQUIRED**

YouTube | ~23 min

[https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dm7f47bTu9M](<https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=dm7f47bTu9M>)

This video traces the history and evolution of the LAN party, highlighting its pivotal role in shaping social interactions within gaming. It explains that while early games were largely single-player, the multiplayer aspect gained prominence, leading to the rise of arcades and, crucially, the LAN party with the release of Doom in 1993. A LAN party, short for Local Area Network party, involved gamers bringing their PCs to a central location to connect and play together, fostering intense social interaction, competition, and shared experiences (often with food and drinks). The video details how games like Quake, Unreal Tournament, Counter-Strike, and RTS games like Age of Empires and Starcraft became LAN staples, forming the "foundations of esports". However, after 2005, LAN parties saw a decline due to the rise of high-speed internet, which allowed online gaming to replicate these social experiences virtually through MMOs, online shooters, and communication software like Discord. Despite the shift to online, the source concludes that LAN parties "walked so that Discord and esports could run," underscoring their lasting influence on modern social gaming gatherings and competitive culture.

**Highlights:**

* **Foundational Role in Social Gaming:** The source clearly establishes LAN parties as a "quintessential way to game" that created entirely new social gatherings, especially after the release of Doom in 1993. This highlights their crucial role in demonstrating gaming's capacity to bring people together in person for shared competitive and cooperative experiences.  
* **Evolution from Physical to Virtual Socialization:** The video effectively chronicles the transition of gaming's social landscape from in-person LAN parties to expansive online multiplayer environments, driven by advancements in internet technology. It explains how modern online features and platforms like Discord evolved to replicate and enhance the community-building aspects once exclusive to LAN parties.  
* **Catalyst for Esports and Modern Gaming Culture:** LAN parties are credited with being the "foundations of esports" and for developing many key aspects of modern gaming culture, such as the "rig war competitions" and leaderboards. This demonstrates their enduring legacy in shaping how competitive and social gaming interactions unfold today.

### What is Toxicity in Games?

**REQUIRED**

Blog Post | ~7 min

[https://indiecator.org/2023/09/27/what-is-toxicity-in-games/](<https://indiecator.org/2023/09/27/what-is-toxicity-in-games/>)

This opinion piece defines "toxicity" in gaming as actions players take to ruin someone else's game or day, including verbal abuse, harassment, hate speech, threats, and in-game behaviors like griefing. The author suggests that much of this toxicity stems from a "sense of entitlement" and a "conflict in expectations" between players (e.g., casual vs. competitive mentalities, "main character syndrome"). The article points out the alarming statistic that 86% of US gamers experienced some form of harassment in 2020, with a significant portion facing severe abuse. A key issue, according to the author, is the normalization and even glorification of toxic behavior by certain streamers and content creators, leading to imitation by young fans. To combat this, the source calls for platforms to enforce their terms of service, developers to provide effective detection and reporting tools (like mute and report functions), and players to actively use these tools and set positive examples.

**Highlights:**

* **Definition and Root Causes of Toxicity:** The source clearly defines "toxicity" as abusive and harmful behaviors intended to "ruin someone else's game or day," attributing its prevalence largely to a "sense of entitlement" and "conflict in expectations" among players. This provides key insights into what causes division within gaming communities.  
* **Normalization and Glorification of Harmful Behavior:** The article critically highlights the "worrying" normalization of toxicity within gaming culture, where it's often dismissed or even "glorified" by some content creators, leading to its imitation by younger audiences. This illustrates how certain aspects of gaming culture can actively perpetuate division and harm.  
* **Multi-stakeholder Call to Action Against Toxicity:** The author proposes a comprehensive strategy to combat toxicity, urging platforms to enforce rules, developers to provide better detection and reporting tools, and players themselves to actively use mute/report features and lead by example. This framework emphasizes collective responsibility in fostering healthier, more cohesive communities.

### Dangerous games : what the moral panic over role-playing games says about play, religion, and imagined worlds by Joseph Laycock

**EXPLORE FURTHER**

Book

[https://maine.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/permalink/01MAINE\_INST/1i25tbk/alma991048182448308681](<https://maine.primo.exlibrisgroup.com/permalink/01MAINE_INST/1i25tbk/alma991048182448308681>)

**Book description:** The 1980's saw the peak of a moral panic over fantasy role-playing games such as Dungeons and Dragons. A coalition of moral entrepreneurs that included representatives from the Christian Right, the field of psychology, and law enforcement claimed that these games were not only psychologically dangerous but an occult religion masquerading as a game. Dangerous Games explores both the history and the sociological significance of this panic. Fantasy role-playing games do share several functions in common with religion. However, religion-as a socially constructed world of shared meaning-can also be compared to a fantasy role-playing game. In fact, the claims of the moral entrepreneurs, in which they presented themselves as heroes battling a dark conspiracy, often resembled the very games of imagination they condemned as evil. By attacking the imagination, they preserved the taken-for-granted status of their own socially constructed reality. Interpreted in this way, the panic over fantasy-role playing games yields new insights about how humans play and together construct and maintain meaningful worlds. Laycock's clear and accessible writing ensures that Dangerous Games will be required reading for those with an interest in religion, popular culture, and social behavior, both in the classroom and beyond.
