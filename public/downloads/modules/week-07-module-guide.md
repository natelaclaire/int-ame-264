### Role of Video Games in Improving Health-Related Outcomes: A Systematic Review

Research Report | ~36 min

[https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3391574/](<https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3391574/>)

This systematic review, conducted in 2010, aimed to determine if video games are effective interventions for improving health outcomes. It analyzed 38 randomized controlled trials (RCTs) out of 1452 retrieved articles, focusing on studies where video games were the intervention and tested a health-promoting, clinically relevant outcome. The review found that video games were evaluated for a wide range of health purposes across all ages, most commonly for physical or psychological therapy. Among the 195 health outcomes examined across all studies, video games showed positive results for 69% of psychological therapy outcomes and 59% of physical therapy outcomes. However, the study noted that the overall quality of the studies was generally poor, with short follow-up periods and limited blinding or clear randomization methods. Despite these limitations, the review concludes that video games show potential for improving health across various areas and demographic groups.
- The review identified 38 Randomized Controlled Trials (RCTs) that tested the effect of video games on clinically relevant health outcomes.
- Video games were found to have improved 69% of psychological therapy outcomes and 59% of physical therapy outcomes across the included studies.
